## The Concrete Dilemma Every Builder Faces

When starting a construction project, one of the earliest decisions is: **mix concrete on-site or order Ready-Mix Concrete (RMC)?** This choice affects your project's quality, timeline, cost, and even legal compliance.

## What Is Ready-Mix Concrete (RMC)?

RMC is concrete that is manufactured in a **centralized batching plant** under controlled conditions and delivered to the construction site in transit mixers. The mix design is computed precisely based on the required grade (M20, M25, M30, etc.).

## Head-to-Head Comparison

| Parameter | RMC | Site-Mixed |
|-----------|-----|-----------|
| **Quality consistency** | ✅ High — computerized batching | ⚠️ Variable — human error |
| **Water-cement ratio** | ✅ Precise | ❌ Often too high |
| **Compressive strength** | ✅ Meets design grade | ⚠️ Often 10-15% below target |
| **Labour required** | ✅ Minimal | ❌ Intensive |
| **Wastage** | ✅ < 2% | ❌ 5-15% typical |
| **Speed** | ✅ Fast delivery | ⚠️ Slower |
| **Minimum order** | ⚠️ Usually 4-6 cubic meters | ✅ Any quantity |
| **Remote sites** | ❌ Limited access | ✅ Feasible |
| **Cost per m³** | Slightly higher upfront | Appears cheaper |

## Why "Cheaper" Site Mix Often Costs More

Site-mixed concrete often suffers from:
1. **Excess water** — workers add water to make mixing easier, reducing strength by up to 20%
2. **Poor aggregate grading** — local sand and gravel without sieve analysis
3. **Inconsistent mix ratios** — manual volume batching vs. weight batching
4. **No admixtures** — missing plasticizers, accelerators, or retarders needed for the job

These deficiencies can lead to **cracks, spalling, and structural failures** that cost far more to repair than the initial savings.

## When to Choose RMC

✅ Slab pours over 10 cubic meters  
✅ Columns and shear walls needing M30+ grade  
✅ Projects with tight timelines  
✅ Any structure requiring IS 456 compliance documentation  
✅ When third-party quality audits are involved  

## When Site Mixing May Be Acceptable

✅ Very small quantities (< 2 m³)  
✅ Remote sites beyond RMC plant range  
✅ Non-structural elements (pathways, garden walls)  

## IS Code Requirements

**IS 456:2000** (Code of Practice for Plain and Reinforced Concrete) recommends RMC for all structural concrete above M20. For high-strength applications, IS 10262 provides mix design guidelines that are practically impossible to follow accurately with site mixing.

## The Hidden Danger in Your Walls

When you paint a room, you can smell the paint for days. That smell is **VOCs — Volatile Organic Compounds** — evaporating into the air you breathe. Studies show that indoor VOC concentrations can be 2-5x higher than outdoor levels, even weeks after painting.

Common VOCs in paint include:
- **Benzene** — known carcinogen
- **Formaldehyde** — irritates eyes, nose, throat
- **Toluene** — causes headaches, dizziness
- **Xylene** — liver and kidney damage with prolonged exposure

## What Are Low-VOC and Zero-VOC Paints?

### Low-VOC Paints
Contain less than **50 g/L of VOCs** (compared to 200-400 g/L in conventional paints). They still have some VOCs from pigments and tints added later, so the actual content may vary.

### Zero-VOC Paints  
Contain **less than 5 g/L** — essentially trace amounts. These are the gold standard for:
- Children's bedrooms
- Hospitals and clinics
- Senior living spaces
- Anyone with asthma or chemical sensitivities

## How to Read a Paint Label

Look for these certifications:
- **Green Seal GS-11** — strict limits on VOCs and toxic ingredients
- **LEED credits** — paints must meet VOC limits for LEED green building certification
- **Eco Mark** — India's official eco-labelling scheme

> Always ask for the **Technical Data Sheet (TDS)** from your paint supplier. It lists exact VOC content.

## Primers Matter Too

Most people focus on the topcoat but ignore primers, which often have **higher VOC content than the paint itself**. Look for:
- Water-based primers (much lower VOCs than oil-based)
- Shellac-based primers only when blocking severe stains

## Practical Tips for a Healthier Paint Job

1. **Ventilate during and after painting** — keep windows open for at least 72 hours
2. **Paint in mild weather** — high humidity slows drying and increases off-gassing time
3. **Apply thin coats** — two thin coats dry faster and off-gas less than one thick coat
4. **Dispose of leftover paint properly** — never pour down drains

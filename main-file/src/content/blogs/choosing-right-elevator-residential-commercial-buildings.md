## Why Elevator Selection Matters

An elevator is not just a convenience — it's a long-term infrastructure investment. A wrong choice leads to high maintenance costs, frequent breakdowns, and unhappy residents or tenants. This guide helps you make an informed decision.

## Key Factors to Evaluate

### 1. Traffic Analysis (Passenger Volume)
The first step is calculating your **peak-hour traffic demand**:
- **Residential buildings**: Typically 5-8% of residents need the elevator within a 5-minute window
- **Commercial buildings**: Up to 12-15% of occupants during morning rush hour

For a 10-floor residential building with 100 flats, a **single 13-person capacity elevator at 1 m/s** is usually sufficient.

### 2. Machine Room vs. Machine-Room-Less (MRL)
| Feature | Traditional (MR) | Machine-Room-Less (MRL) |
|---------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Space required | Needs top-floor machine room | No extra space |
| Energy use | Higher | 30-40% lower |
| Maintenance | More frequent | Less frequent |
| Cost | Lower initial | Higher initial, lower lifetime |

**Recommendation**: For new constructions, always go MRL.

### 3. Speed Selection
- **2-5 floors**: 0.6 - 1.0 m/s
- **6-15 floors**: 1.0 - 1.5 m/s  
- **16+ floors**: 1.75 - 2.5 m/s

> Going higher than needed wastes money. Consult your structural engineer for the right speed.

### 4. Safety Certifications
Look for lifts complying with:
- **IS 14665** (Indian Standard for electric traction lifts)
- **EN 81-20/50** (European standard, widely adopted in India)
- CE marking for imported components

### 5. Energy Efficiency
Modern VVVF (Variable Voltage Variable Frequency) drives reduce energy consumption by up to 40% compared to older relay-logic systems. Regenerative drives can even feed power back to the building grid.
